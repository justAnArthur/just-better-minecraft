# 2.6 Clipmap streaming of LOD rings, skirts at seams

**Goal:** the player sees kilometres of terrain.
- The server streams rings of LOD cubes around each player. Every level is a disk twice as far and twice as coarse as the one inside it.
- The client meshes and draws them with the existing renderer. Each volume is drawn by exactly one level: the finest one that is ready there.
- Short skirts hide the cracks where levels meet.

Flying across ring borders shows no holes.

LOD content comes from the server's pyramid. Real terrain arrives through 2.4's downsampling. Unvisited terrain arrives through 2.7's worldgen sampling, which runs in parallel and lands behind the same interface (see Parallel work).

## Scope

### Rings on the server (`streaming.rs`, `server.rs`: `Player`, `stream_player`, a new `stream_player_lod`, `phase_streaming` step 2)

- **Per player and per level** *n* in `1..=lod_levels`: a level center (the level-*n* cube containing the player), a sent set, and the walk state.
- **Shapes.** The desired set at level *n* is `desired_offsets(lod_view_radius, lod_vertical_radius)` around the level-*n* center, in level-*n* cubes. The keep set is the same shape at +1 in both radii (hysteresis), as for LOD0.
  - Each level is a **full disk**, not an annulus. The client needs the coarse cube under a finer one while the finer one loads, and to fill octants the finer level does not cover yet.
  - The cost is about a quarter more cubes per level; report it.
- **Order.** One merged walk over `(level, offset)`, sorted by the offset's squared distance in level units, then by level **descending** (coarse first at equal distance), then by `(x, y, z)`. Precompute it once from the config.
- **Sending.**
  - A cube whose `ready` is `Some` is sent as `LodCubeData` and recorded in the sent set.
  - A cube that is not ready is requested (`request`, while `in_flight() < lod_max_in_flight`) and **skipped**. Unlike LOD0, the LOD walk does not stop at a missing cube: the client's coverage rule (below) makes any arrival order hole-free.
  - At most `lod_send_budget_per_tick` LOD messages per player per tick, re-sends included.
- **Re-sends.**
  - Once per tick, before the per-player pass, drain `take_changed()`. Every changed cube that a player has in its sent set is queued for that player.
  - Queued re-sends go first within the budget, and the queue persists across ticks, so nothing starves.
  - A re-send replaces the client's copy. An edit's LOD change reaches a player within one tick when the budget is not saturated.
- **Unloading.** When a level center changes, send `UnloadLodCube` for every sent cube that left that level's keep set.
- **Cost contract,** like 2.4's LOD0 one:
  - A settled player whose rings are complete costs a few lookups per level per tick, with no scans of sent or desired sets.
  - While cubes are missing, the per-tick scan is bounded. For example, keep a cursor at the first unsent entry plus a bounded skip window. Document the bound.
- **Off switches.** No LOD messages at all when `streaming` is false, `lod_levels == 0`, or the player is not joined.
- **Eviction** of world (LOD0) cubes is unchanged. LOD cubes are not evicted from the pyramid (2.7's Known issue).

### The client's LOD world (`world.rs` or a new `lod.rs`)

- Store LOD cubes per level, beside the LOD0 cubes, with the same version and dirty bookkeeping.
  - `LodCubeData` inserts or replaces the cube and dirties it and its loaded same-level neighbors.
  - `UnloadLodCube` removes it.
  - Mesh inputs carry the 26 **same-level** neighbors.
  - LOD0 behavior is unchanged.
- Give mesh jobs, mesh results and GPU residency a key that includes the level. A `(level, CubePos)` key type is fine; LOD0 is level 0.
- **Ready.** A cube at any level is *ready* when it is loaded and its current mesh is uploaded. An empty mesh counts: a uniform-air or fully buried cube is ready once meshed. A re-mesh in flight keeps the old mesh, so the cube stays ready.
- **Mesh scheduling.** LOD0 dirties keep priority (edits first, then nearest). LOD cubes are taken after them, nearest-first by distance in blocks divided by 2ⁿ, within the same per-frame budget. Starvation-free: report the budget split you choose.

### Coverage: exactly one level per volume (pure logic, unit-testable)

For each cube *c*, `drawn(c)` is defined recursively, top level first:

- `drawn(c)` holds iff `ready(c)`, and either `drawn(parent(c))`, or no ancestor of *c* is ready.
- A drawn cube at level *n ≥ 1* **masks** octant *o* when the child at *o* (level *n−1*) is ready. A ready child of a drawn cube is itself drawn.
- A drawn cube draws exactly its unmasked octants. A drawn cube with all 8 octants masked draws nothing and emits no commands.

**Property** (prove it with the tests): every point contained in some ready cube is drawn by exactly one cube. No overlap, no hole.
- Points contained in no ready cube are not drawn.
- Trace why for the docs: along a point's chain of cubes, the topmost ready one is drawn. Drawing passes down while the chain stays ready, and stops at the first cube that is not ready, whose parent draws the point.

**Update it incrementally.**
- A readiness change of `(n, p)` can change the mask of its parent, the drawn state of its descendants, and its own state.
- The update cost is proportional to the affected subtree, never a full rescan per frame. A frame with no readiness change does no coverage work.
- Expose a from-scratch recomputation for tests, and assert that incremental equals from-scratch.

The transient this rule allows is accepted: a coarse cube can briefly cover a finer one when the cube between them arrives late, typically for a moment at startup. Document it.

### Meshing LOD cubes (`mesh/greedy.rs`)

- **Same mesher, level-aware.** Cells are 2ⁿ blocks, but the mesher works in cells, so the face format is unchanged.
- **Octant-split faces.** At level ≥ 1, greedy merging never crosses the 16-cell octant planes (x, y or z = `CUBE_SIZE/2`). Every face then lies in exactly one octant, which masking needs. LOD0 meshing is unchanged, bit for bit; the goldens prove it.
- **Skirts (level ≥ 1, side directions ±X/±Z only).**
  - Emit c's face in direction *d* even though the cell across *d* is filled, when all of these hold:
    - c is filled;
    - the cell across *d* lies in a **different octant or a different cube**;
    - that cell is filled, or is in a missing neighbor cube;
    - c is one of the top **two** filled cells of its column run: the cell above c is air, or the cell two above is.
  - These faces stand in for the coarse cell's side when the octant (or cube) across is drawn by finer cubes whose surface is lower. The rule leans solid, so a coarse surface sits up to 2ⁿ − 1 blocks above the fine one. When that octant is drawn by the same coarse cube, the faces sit inside terrain and are hidden by the neighbor's top face.
  - Skirt faces are ordinary faces, so greedy merging applies within the octant rule. Explain the rule in the mesher docs with a diagram in a comment.
  - Report the skirt face overhead on a surface LOD cube.
- **AO** comes from same-level neighbors, as at LOD0.

### Rendering (`render/chunks.rs`, `terrain.wgsl`, `cull.wgsl`)

- **CubeMeta flags** (`start_flags.y`) gain the level (bits 8–11) and the octant mask (bits 16–23).
  - Keep the 80-byte layout and update the layout test.
  - Meta is rewritten only when the level, mask or mesh changes, never per frame.
- **One origin formula,** scaled: `origin = (pos·2ⁿ − camera_cube)·CUBE_SIZE − camera_frac`.
  - Shader, cull shader and CPU (`cube_origin`) share it exactly.
  - Cell offsets are scaled by 2ⁿ before the add.
  - Check that `pos·2ⁿ` cannot overflow i32 for any position the server can stream, and assert it.
- **Textures:** UVs stay one tile per **block**: multiply by 2ⁿ, so textures line up across ring borders. Mips already exist.
- **Masked octants:** the vertex shader collapses faces whose owning cell is in a masked octant. Emit a degenerate position for all six vertices, so they produce no fragments.
- **Culling, every path:**
  - AABBs are `CUBE_SIZE·2ⁿ` wide.
  - Direction skipping uses the scaled AABB.
  - Cubes not drawn under the coverage rule, fully masked cubes included, emit **no** commands on cpu-cull, gpu-cull or fallback. On gpu-cull, clear their flag so the compute pass skips them.
  - The three paths stay pixel-identical, LOD scenes included.
- **Paths:** keep 2.5's `select_path` and the cpu-cull default. **Re-measure gpu-cull** with rings, where geometry grows a lot. If gpu-cull now wins at game scale, report it with numbers, but do not flip the default here.

## Tests

**Server rings** (crates/server tests, inline mode, deterministic):
- After settling, every `(level, pos)` in the desired disks whose `ready` is `Some` was sent exactly once, and nothing outside the desired disks was sent.
- Messages respect the per-tick budget. The order follows the merged walk.
- Moving the player across a level-2 boundary unloads exactly the cubes that left the level-2 keep set, and sends the new ones.
- **Edit re-send:** an edit near the player re-sends every changed LOD cube the player has, within one tick, and the content equals `lod_cube` on the server.
- Re-sends never starve under a saturated budget: a fake stream of changes, with a bounded wait.
- No LOD messages with `lod_levels = 0`, with `streaming = false`, or to a pending connection.
- A settled idle player gets zero LOD messages per tick for 100 ticks.

**Coverage** (client unit tests, pure):
- **Property test.** 10,000 seeded random readiness sets over levels 0..=4 on a small region, including negative coordinates. For every LOD0 cell (or every cell of the finest level in play) in the region, the number of drawn cubes covering it is exactly 1 if some ready cube contains it, else 0.
- Incremental updates under random readiness toggles equal the from-scratch result after every step.
- A frame with no change does zero work. Count it.

**Mesher:**
- At level ≥ 1, no face crosses an octant plane: check every face of a random-terrain LOD cube.
- At LOD0 the output is unchanged, byte for byte, against the pre-change mesher on 20 seeded cubes. Keep a copy of the old function in the test, or compare against stored hashes.
- **Skirts:** a step terrain whose surface drops by one cell across an octant plane emits the skirt on the higher side, two cells deep. A flat surface across the plane emits skirts whose faces sit under the neighbor's top face. Assert the cells directly.

**Rendering** (offscreen, skip without an adapter like the other GPU tests):
- **Scale:** a single LOD1 cube built from a known LOD0 pattern renders like that pattern at LOD0 scaled ×2 (a simple shape, compared directly), on every path.
- **Masking:** a LOD1 cube with one octant masked shows the sentinel where that octant's terrain was, and a fully masked cube emits no draws on any path (stats).
- **Rings scene, client-only and deterministic.**
  - Build a synthetic heightfield and fill levels 0..=3 around a camera. Fill each level by evaluating the heightfield at that level's resolution, exactly as a ring would arrive, with the downsampling rule applied to level-0 data where the footprint is small.
  - Feed the cubes through the client world, the coverage rule, the mesher and the renderer.
  - Use 8 cameras, among them close, zoomed and steep-down views straight at a LOD0/LOD1 border, a LOD1/LOD2 border, and a corner where three levels meet.
  - Each camera: **zero sentinel pixels** below the horizon line, minus a margin you compute and document from the camera pitch and FOV.
  - Save the images under `target/` for inspection and add goldens for 3 of them. The paths are pixel-identical on this scene.
- All existing goldens pass unchanged.

**Fly test** (crates/game, real server with worker threads, needs 2.7 merged):
