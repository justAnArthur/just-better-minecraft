What this says:

1. **It is pixels, not geometry.** At 640×360 the frame is 1.65 ms whatever the shaders do: that is the vertex/geometry floor of ~1.2–2.1 M faces (6 vertices each, pulled). From 2560×1440 to 5120×2880 (4× the pixels) the frame grows ~3×.
2. **MSAA 4× costs ~10 ms of the 5K frame** (≈15 → 5.3 ms min). On a tile-based GPU most of that is not the multisampling itself but **bandwidth from splitting the scene over passes**: today every frame runs a clear pass, the terrain pass, the sky pass, the debug-lines and outline passes when shown, and an empty resolve pass. Each pass loads and stores the 4× color and the 4× depth — about 236 MB per attachment per load or store at 5K — so the scene moves well over a gigabyte per frame through memory. The sky pass alone (one fullscreen triangle, Equal depth test) costs ~8 ms at 5K for that reason: its shading is cheap, its load and store of two 4× attachments is not.
3. **The terrain fragment shader is ~4 ms at 5K** (`flat_fs`), the fog/haze ~3 ms and the clouds ~1 ms. The terrain shader's `discard` (cutout layers) also turns off Apple's hidden-surface removal for every terrain fragment, so overdraw is shaded in full.
4. **Culling is not the lever here.** cpu-cull draws ~3.5 k commands in 2–3 multi-draws; occlusion would remove little from this open view (the ground hides far terrain only behind ridges), and gpu-cull's Hi-Z build costs ~3 ms at 5K by itself. The CPU (`render()` 1–3.5 ms) is within budget.
5. **Per-pass timestamps overlap on Apple** (a pass's span includes waiting on the previous pass's fragments: the empty resolve pass reads as long as the sky pass), so the per-pass columns are indicative only; the frame span and the ablations are what to trust.

The user's HUD shows 2.3 M faces and 3804 commands where the harness shows 1.2 M and 3494 at the same eye (1.29 M and 3695 at 5120×2560): a wider window widens the horizontal field of view, but not by that much. The rest is likely the in-flight world (cubes still meshing, or not yet replaced by finer levels) where the harness measures a settled one; the frame's cost is in pixels either way.

Plan, in order: (a) one scene pass with the resolve as its store action; (b) a render scale with an auto default for very high pixel counts; (c) cheaper fragments (front-to-back order, an opaque pipeline without `discard`); then re-measure.
