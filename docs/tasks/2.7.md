# 2.7 LOD from worldgen for unvisited terrain, stored LOD in redb

**Goal:** every LOD cube the server is asked for becomes ready quickly, whether or not anyone has been there.
- Octants backed by real terrain (LOD0 cubes that have been in the world) come from the 2.4 downsampling, as now.
- Every other octant is sampled straight from the worldgen density graph at that level's resolution. No full-detail cube is generated for it.
- Ready LOD cubes are stored in redb, so after a restart the far view is back at once, without sampling worldgen again.

Clipmap streaming, the protocol and rendering are 2.6, which runs in parallel and calls the pyramid only through the interface below.

## Scope

### The content model (write it in the `lod.rs` module docs)

The content of a LOD cube at level *n* and position *p* is defined per octant *o*:
- **Real**, when the child at level *n−1* holds real data. At level 1 that is a LOD0 cube that has been in the world, in its latest content. At level *n ≥ 2* it is a level-(*n−1*) child with at least one real octant.
  - The octant is the downsample of the child's **full content**, worldgen-filled octants included. Never placeholder air.
  - `known` bit *o* is set exactly for the real octants.
- **Worldgen**, otherwise: the octant of `Generator::sample_lod(n, p)` (below).

A cube is **ready** when every octant has content under this model: real, or filled from worldgen (or loaded from storage).
- `ready(level, pos)` returns `Some` only then.
- The 2.4 stub returned partial cubes whose unknown octants read as air. That ends here, and so does the 2.4 note that "a partially known child propagates its placeholder air". Update both docs.

This model is a pure function of two inputs: the set of LOD0 cubes ever seen with their latest content, and the worldgen. Extend `LodPyramid::rebuild` into the reference implementation of it.
- Inputs: a generator and the LOD0 cubes. Output: every ready cube from scratch.
- The incremental pyramid must equal the reference exactly, cell for cell and `known` for `known`, after any order of inserts, edits, requests, fills and storage loads. This is the main correctness property, as in 2.4.

Consequence: a real octant at level *n ≥ 2* can be computed only once its child is ready.
- When a level-*n* cube gains or changes a real octant and is not ready yet, it must be filled first. Defer the propagation up until it is ready.
- It must never be propagated with placeholder air.
- Document how the pending state works and prove it with the tests.

### Worldgen LOD sampling (`terrain.rs`)

`Generator::sample_lod(level: u8, pos: CubePos) -> Cube`, for `level ≥ 1`, in the same cell format as the pyramid:

1. **Fast paths from `bounds`,** as `generate` does: uniform air or uniform stone without sampling. Reuse the same lookahead rule for stone.
2. **Otherwise sample the child grid.**
   - Sample density on the level-(*n−1*) grid: 64³ points at step 2^(*n−1*), each at the minimum corner of its child cell. Add the surface lookahead above.
   - Run the surface stage in **block units**: a sample's run `d` is the number of consecutive solid samples above it times 2^(*n−1*), capped at 4, and its `y` is its block y. So at coarse levels the dirt band vanishes and the top stays grass or sand.
   - Then apply `downsample_cell` to each 2×2×2 group.
   - **Level 1 is then exact:** it equals `downsample_octant` of the eight generated LOD0 cubes. Assert it in a test.
3. Use the graph's band/heightmap fast path where it applies, as `generate` does. Do not evaluate the full 3D graph per sample when the band covers it. Measure it.

This is the "LOD sampled straight from worldgen" of the plan. It never calls `generate` and never touches the LOD0 world.

### Fill pipeline (`lod.rs`, new `lod_fill.rs` if it helps, `gen_pool.rs`)

- `request(level, pos)`:
  - Deduplicated, and capped by the caller through `in_flight()`.
  - It queues a **load-or-sample** job: the stored record when there is one (below), otherwise `sample_lod`.
  - A request for a ready cube, or a level outside `1..=max_levels`, does nothing.
  - Cubes the pyramid needs for its own pending propagation (see the content model) are requested internally. They count in `in_flight()` too.
- **Workers:** the jobs run off the tick thread.
  - Either extend `GenPool` with a LOD job kind or add a small pool. Choose one and say why.
  - **Rule:** LOD jobs never delay LOD0 jobs. A worker takes queued LOD0 work first.
  - Keep the 2.3 guarantees: named threads, panics re-raised on the tick thread, drop joins every worker promptly with no hang, even with a deep LOD backlog. Add drop-guard tests for the new queue.
  - **Inline mode** (`gen_threads == 0`): `poll()` runs up to `gen_budget_per_tick` LOD jobs synchronously, after the LOD0 budget. Tests and benches use it, so it must be deterministic.
- `poll()` collects finished jobs and fills each cube's non-real octants from the job's result.
  - It never overwrites a real octant: real data that arrived while the job ran wins.
  - It completes the pending propagations that became possible, and records every changed or newly ready cube for `take_changed`.
- `take_changed()` reports a cube when it **becomes ready** and on every later content change. It does not report changes to cubes that are not ready, so 2.6 never streams partial cubes.

### Stored LOD (`storage.rs`, the save path in `server.rs`)

- **Keys:** LOD cubes go into the existing `cubes` table under key kind = level (1..=4), the scheme 2.3 reserved. LOD0 keys stay unchanged.
- **Record:** `[schema 2][known u8][zstd frame]` of the same core codec. Schema 1 stays the LOD0 record. A LOD record that fails to decode is logged and treated as absent. Never panic.
- **What is stored:** every ready LOD cube, once when it becomes ready and again after each content change.
  - Batched through the existing jbm-save thread and its 5 s interval. Reuse `SaveBatch`; add a LOD section to it.
  - LOD records derived from an edit are saved **in the same transaction** as the LOD0 edit they come from, so a crash never leaves stored LOD disagreeing with stored LOD0 edits.
- **Loading** is part of the job (load-or-sample).
  - A stored cube's real octants are real, and its other octants are filled.
  - If memory already holds real octants for it (LOD0 cubes arrived first), memory wins for those octants. The result must still equal the reference.
- **Consistency after restart.** Unedited LOD0 cubes regenerate identically, and their downsample equals what the stored real octants hold. So a restart changes nothing. Test it.
- **Size:** report the database size and record count after streaming the default rings around spawn.
- **Format version:** the meta table's format version stays 1 if old databases still open unchanged (they have no kind ≥ 1 keys). Say so in the storage docs.
- **Worldgen changes:** stored LOD from a different worldgen would be stale. Store a worldgen fingerprint in meta (a hash of the compiled graph and seed). On mismatch, ignore and overwrite stored LOD records. Never delete LOD0 records.

## Tests

**Sampling:**
- Level 1 from `sample_lod` equals the downsample of the eight generated LOD0 cubes, exactly, at 20 seeded positions. Include negative coordinates and a cube straddling `SAND_LEVEL`.
- **Levels 2, 3 and 4 against the exact downsample** of fully generated LOD0 terrain. Use 30 seeded surface positions for level 2 and 10 for levels 3 and 4; level 4 is a 16³-cube footprint, so keep it to a few.
  - For every column, compare the top filled cell. At least **95%** of columns must be within ±1 cell, and at least **90%** must share the top material.
  - Print the actual numbers per level. These bound the visible pop when a worldgen guess is replaced by real data.
- The bounds fast paths: a cube far above the terrain is uniform air and one far below is uniform stone, both with zero `sample_grid` calls. Count them through a test hook.
- `sample_lod` never calls `generate`. Assert it with a counter or a test hook.

**Content model:**
- **Incremental equals reference.** Use a seeded random interleaving over a 4×2×4-cube LOD0 region and its level-1..4 ancestors:
  - LOD0 inserts, edits (2.4's edit generator, cube borders and negative coordinates included), requests at random levels, and polls.
  - In inline mode, after every 100 operations, every ready cube equals the reference cell for cell and `known` for `known`.
  - Take the seed from an env var, defaulting to a fixed one. It passes with 10 different seeds.
- **No placeholder air:** visit only half of a level-2 cube's footprint, then request it. Its unvisited octants equal the worldgen sample, and its visited octants equal the downsample of the real level-1 children (themselves filled). Assert the cells directly.
- `ready` is `None` while a cube is pending, and `take_changed` never reports a cube that is not ready.
- An edit under a ready level-3 cube reaches level 3, and `take_changed` reports levels 1–3.
- Real data wins: insert a LOD0 cube while its parent's sample job is in flight (threaded mode, held with a test hook). The real octant survives `poll`.

**Storage:**
- **Reload is instant.** Request the default rings around spawn (the 2.6 disks: `lod_view_radius` and `lod_vertical_radius` at every level) through `Server::request_lod`, and poll until all are ready. Drop the server; it flushes. Reopen it and request the same cubes.
  - After the first `poll` in inline mode, or within one second threaded, every cube is ready.
  - `lod_samples()` stayed 0.
  - The cubes equal the ones before the restart.
  - Report both times: ready from worldgen and ready from storage.
- **Edits survive in LOD:** edit a pillar, flush, reopen, and request the ancestors. They show the edit without the LOD0 cube being loaded.
- A corrupt LOD record is resampled, never a panic.
- A changed worldgen fingerprint ignores stored LOD.
- Kill-during-save, as in 2.3's test: the reopened world's LOD records decode and agree with its LOD0 records.
- The far terrain needs no full detail. After the rings are ready around spawn, `world_cube_count()` equals what LOD0 streaming alone loads, with no extra LOD0 generation.

**Workers:**
- LOD0 priority. With a deep LOD backlog queued, a LOD0 request completes within the time of a few LOD0 generations. Hold the workers with a test hook; do not use timing thresholds that flake under load.
- Drop with a deep LOD backlog returns promptly. Panics in a LOD job reach the tick thread.

All 2.4 tests keep passing. Update the ones whose meaning the content model changes (partial cubes are no longer ready, rebuild takes a generator) and say which ones in the report.
