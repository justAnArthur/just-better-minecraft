# 2.4 LOD pyramid: downsampling rule and edit propagation

**Goal:** the server keeps a pyramid of LOD cubes built from the real world.
- Every LOD0 cube that enters the world (generated or loaded) is downsampled into its LOD1 parent, then up to the top level.
- Every accepted edit updates the one affected cell per level, stopping as soon as a level does not change.
- The downsampling rule keeps thin features: a one-block wall survives LOD1 and LOD2.

Nothing is streamed or rendered yet. Clipmap streaming is 2.6; LOD sampled from worldgen and stored LOD are 2.7.

## Scope

### Downsampling rule (`jbm-core`, new `lod.rs`, pure logic)

A level-*n+1* cell covers a 2×2×2 group of level-*n* cells, its "children". Children are indexed `c = dx + 2·dz + 4·dy` (same order as `LocalPos::index`). The rule is a pure function of the eight child states:

`pub fn downsample_cell(children: [BlockStateId; 8]) -> BlockStateId`

1. **Occupancy.** A child is *filled* when it is not `AIR`. The cell is filled when **at least 2** children are filled; otherwise it is `AIR`.
   - So a 1×1 pillar (2 of 8) and a one-block wall (4 of 8) survive at every level, while a single floating block or a torch-sized feature vanishes.
   - The rule leans solid: surfaces can rise by up to 2ⁿ − 1 blocks and tunnels narrower than 2ⁿ can close. Downward skirts (2.6) hide the first. The second is invisible from LOD distance. Write this in the module docs.
2. **Material**, for a filled cell, is "what you see from above":
   - The candidates are the topmost filled child of each of the four `(dx, dz)` columns of the group. A column with no filled child gives none.
   - Pick the most common state among the candidates.
   - **Ties** go to the state that appears at the highest `dy`, then to the smallest `BlockStateId`.
   - Effect: grass stays on top of a surface whichever way the surface lines up with the group, and a wall standing on stone ground keeps its own material. Put one worked example for each in the docs.
   - This replaces design.md's looser "prefer a solid block visible from outside". Update that paragraph in docs/design.md to state this rule (occupancy ≥ 2 of 8, material from the column tops).

The rule looks only at the eight children, never at cells outside the group, so one edit changes at most one cell per level. That locality is what makes edit propagation exact. Say so in the docs, and do not "improve" the rule by looking at neighbors.

Water and every other non-air state count as filled. Block properties (solid, opaque) are not consulted. A later task may refine the rule with registry data; it must then stay local.

### Cube-level downsampling (`jbm-core::lod`)

- **Octants:** a child cube at level *n* fills one octant (a `CUBE_SIZE/2` cube of cells) of its parent at level *n+1*.
  - `octant = dx + 2·dz + 4·dy`, where `(dx, dy, dz)` is the child position `rem_euclid(2)` per axis.
  - Parent position is `div_euclid(2)` per axis.
  - Add `pub fn parent(pos: CubePos) -> (CubePos, u8)` and `pub fn octant_offset(octant: u8) -> [u8; 3]`.
- **`downsample_region(child: &Cube, parent: &mut Cube, octant: u8, region: CellBox) -> Option<CellBox>`:**
  - `region` is a box of **child** cells (inclusive min, exclusive max, in `0..CUBE_SIZE`) that changed.
  - Recompute every parent cell whose 2×2×2 group overlaps `region`.
  - Return the box of **parent** cells that actually changed, or `None` if none did.
  - The returned box is the next level's input, so propagation stays proportional to what changed.
- **`downsample_octant(child, parent, octant)`** is `downsample_region` over the whole child. It has fast paths:
  - A uniform child fills its octant with that state (`downsample_cell` of eight equal states is that state; assert it in a test).
  - A parent that is uniform and equal to that fill is left untouched, so it is never unpacked.
- **Uniform parents stay cheap:** do not unpack a uniform parent when nothing changes. Call `Cube::compact` after a write that may have made the parent uniform again or shrunk its palette. Document when you call it, and keep its cost in the bench.
- `CellBox` is a small `Copy` type in `lod.rs` with `min: [u8; 3]`, `max: [u8; 3]`, `union` and `single(LocalPos)`.

### The pyramid (`jbm-server`, new `lod.rs`)

- **`LodPyramid`** holds levels `1..=max_levels`, each a `HashMap<CubePos, LodCube>`. Use the core's fast hasher if it is public, else the std one.
  - `LodCube { cube: Cube, known: u8 }`. `known` is an octant bitmask: bit *o* is set once that octant's child has been downsampled into it at least once.
  - Octants never written read as `AIR`. 2.7 fills unknown octants from worldgen. Document the contract: `known` says which octants hold real data, and nothing else may be assumed about unknown ones.
  - A level-*n+1* octant is marked known as soon as its level-*n* child has any known data. A partially known child propagates its air for its unknown octants; document that this is a deliberate simplification for 2.7 to revisit.
- **`insert_lod0(pos, &Cube) -> LodChanges`** downsamples the cube into its level-1 parent (creating it as uniform air if absent), sets the known bit, then propagates the changed region up the levels until a level reports no change or the top is reached.
- **`edit_lod0(pos: BlockPos, cube: &Cube) -> LodChanges`** is the per-edit path. It runs `downsample_region` on the single changed cell, so each level costs one cell, and stops early.
- **`LodChanges`** lists the `(level, CubePos)` cubes whose content changed, in level order. 2.6 will stream exactly these. Tests use it now.
- **Accessors:** `get(level, pos) -> Option<&LodCube>`, `len(level)`, `total_cubes()`, `heap_bytes()`.
- **Full rebuild,** for tests: `LodPyramid::rebuild(max_levels, lod0: impl Iterator<Item = (CubePos, &Cube)>)` builds every level from scratch, bottom up.
  - Incremental results must equal the rebuild exactly, cell for cell and `known` for `known`. This is the main correctness property; see Tests.

### Server integration (`server.rs`)

- `ServerConfig` gains `lod_levels: u8`, the top level kept, with default **4** (LOD1–LOD4). `0` disables the pyramid entirely (no allocation, no work). `protocol_only()` uses 0.
- **Ingest:** in `phase_streaming`, call `insert_lod0` only when `entry()` actually inserts. A stale generated copy that is dropped because an edited cube is already present must not touch the pyramid.
- **Edits:** `apply_edit` calls `edit_lod0` after setting the block.
- **Eviction does not touch the pyramid.** LOD cubes outlive their LOD0 children. That is the point: player builds stay visible from far away.
  - Re-inserting a regenerated or reloaded cube must report no changes. Test it.
  - The pyramid grows with the explored area. Record this in docs/progress.md Known issues as "LOD pyramid has no eviction until 2.6 (rings) and 2.7 (stored LOD)".
- **Accessors:** `Server::lod_cube(level, pos) -> Option<&Cube>`, `Server::lod_known(level, pos) -> Option<u8>`, `Server::lod_cube_count()`.
- **Cost on the tick thread:** `insert_lod0` runs on the tick thread for every collected cube. Its cost is bounded by the bench below. If the surface-cube insert median exceeds **25%** of `worldgen/surface_cube`, report it, and propose moving the level-1 downsample to the gen pool workers. Do not move it in this task.

## Tests

**Rule (core unit tests):**
- All air → air. One filled → air. Two filled (any pair, all 28) → filled.
- Eight equal states → that state.
- **Surface material:**
  - Grass on top, dirt below, all eight filled → grass.
  - Grass bottom layer with air above → grass.
  - A one-step slope (the example in the docs) → grass.
  - Three columns grass-topped and one column whose grass is above the group (dirt at both `dy`) → grass.
- **Wall material:** a planks column pair (`dx = 0`, both `dy`) next to stone ground with air above it (`dx = 1`) → planks.
- **Tie-breaks:** both rules, one case each.
- **Reference check:** 10,000 seeded random 8-tuples over 4 states (air among them) against a brute-force reference written plainly in the test. This guards against map-iteration order leaking into the result.

**Thin features**, the plan's acceptance core, built with `Cube::from_fn` at LOD0 and pushed through `LodPyramid`:
- **One-block wall:** a vertical wall 1 block thick, 32 long and 16 tall, at x = 7, then at x = 8 (both parities). At LOD1 and LOD2, assert the wall cells **directly**: a filled column at the wall's cell x for every covered y and z, with air on both sides.
  - Do not compare images or counts; assert the cells.
- A 1×1 tree trunk (log, 8 tall) survives LOD1 and LOD2, and keeps its log material where it is exposed.
- A single floating block vanishes at LOD1. Document it as the expected behavior.
- **Real terrain:** stream the default view around spawn through `Server` (inline generation). Of the LOD1 and LOD2 columns whose LOD0 columns are all grass-topped, at least **90%** have grass as their top filled cell. Print the actual fraction; steep spots are the expected exceptions.

**Propagation:**
- **Edit shows in the parent LOD:** build a 2×2×2 pillar of planks on the surface through `Server` edits, the real path. Then `lod_cube(1, parent)` and `lod_cube(2, grandparent)` show planks at the right cells.
- Removing the pillar restores the previous cells exactly.
- A material-only edit (replace a 2×2 grass patch with planks) changes the LOD1 cell's material.
- **Incremental equals rebuild:** a seeded random sequence of 2,000 edits over a 4×2×4 region of generated cubes, with `lod_levels = 4`. After every 100 edits the incremental pyramid equals `LodPyramid::rebuild` from the current LOD0 cubes exactly, every cell and every `known` mask. Include edits on cube borders and at negative coordinates.
- Early stop: an edit that changes no LOD1 cell (breaking one block inside solid stone) reports no `LodChanges` and touches no higher level.
- Re-inserting an unchanged cube reports no changes, and so does eviction followed by regeneration through `Server`.
- `lod_levels = 0`: no LOD cubes exist after streaming and edits.
- **Negative coordinates:** parent and octant math for cubes at −1, −2 and −3 on each axis (`div_euclid`), and a wall straddling x = 0.
- **`cube16`:** the core tests pass under `--features cube16`. Run them; the octant size is `CUBE_SIZE/2`.
