# 2.9 Distant-Horizons-style far look: flat material colors and distance fog

**Goal:** the far view reads as a smooth landscape, not as noisy repeated block textures, and the switch between LOD levels is invisible.
- LOD cubes (level ≥ 1) draw each face in a flat per-material color instead of sampling the texture.
- Up close, LOD0 keeps its textures. With distance, LOD0 fades to the same flat colors, so by a ring border both sides look the same.
- Sky-colored distance fog blends the far rings into the horizon.

Reference look: the Distant Horizons mod for Minecraft, whose far LODs are flat-colored, softly shaded and fogged. The user play-tested 2.6 and found the textured far LOD "looks slightly bad"; this fixes that.

## Scope

### Per-layer average colors (`texture_array.rs`, `appearance.rs`)

- At texture load, compute each layer's average color **in linear space**, from the 1×1 mip or by averaging the base layer, weighting by alpha so cutout holes in leaves and glass do not darken it.
- Store the averages in a small storage or uniform buffer, indexed by texture layer, bound in the terrain group.
- Expose them on the CPU for tests.
- Grass top and grass side are different layers, so a hillside reads green on top and earthy on the sides, as in Distant Horizons. Water uses its layer's average.

### Shading (`terrain.wgsl`)

Define one function, `far_color(layer, uv, dist, level)`, documented in the shader, that returns the base color before shade and fog:
- **Level ≥ 1:** the layer's average color. No texture sample, no cutout discard (a LOD cell is solid by the downsampling rule).
- **Level 0:** `mix(texture_sample, average, fade(dist))`.
  - `fade` is a smoothstep from `FADE_START` to `FADE_END` blocks.
  - Defaults: start at about 40% of the LOD0 ring radius, end at about 85% (the LOD0 disk is 8 cubes = 256 blocks). By the LOD0/LOD1 border both sides are flat-colored.
  - Take the distance from the camera-relative position; it is exact enough.
  - The cutout discard still applies to LOD0 texture samples while `fade < 1`.
- **Keep directional face shading and AO** on every level. AO on coarse levels may be softened, since its contrast scales with cell size and looks blotchy far away. If you soften it, use one documented factor per level.
- **Optional subtle variation:** a tiny per-cell brightness jitter (±3%) hashed from the cell's world position, so huge flat areas do not look plastic. Deterministic and identical on all paths. Do it only if it visibly helps, and show before and after in the report.

### Distance fog (`terrain.wgsl`, sky colors in `renderer.rs`)

- Fog color = the **sky color along the view ray at the horizon**: the same gradient function as `sky.wgsl`, evaluated for the fragment's direction with its y clamped to just above 0. Distant terrain then dissolves into the sky behind it with no visible band.
  - Factor the gradient into a function that both shaders share as the same text. WGSL has no includes: concatenate a common snippet at pipeline creation, or keep two copies guarded by a test that compares the source strings.
- **Fog factor:** exponential-squared or a smoothstep on distance.
  - Starts at about 35% of the outermost ring radius (4 levels × 8 cubes; the LOD4 disk reaches about 4 km), and reaches 100% just inside it, so the ring edge is never visible.
  - Compute the radius from a uniform the app sets: the furthest ring radius in blocks, from `ServerConfig`-like values the client knows (the levels it has seen and the ring radius, both in `TerrainUniforms`).
  - Document the formula.
- **Test hook:** `Renderer::set_fog(enabled)` / a `JBM_NO_FOG=1` environment variable, so screenshot tests can isolate geometry from fog. The **sentinel** hole tests run with fog on: fog paints terrain fragments only, so a hole still shows the sentinel sky. Verify it.

### Every path identical

cpu-cull, gpu-cull and fallback share the fragment shader, so they stay pixel-identical. Keep the path-identity tests green.

## Tests

- **Average colors:** a synthetic 2×2 texture layer of known colors averages to the expected linear value. A cutout layer ignores its transparent pixels.
- **Level ≥ 1 is flat:** render a single LOD1 cube of one material (no fog). Every lit top-face pixel is within ±1 LSB of `average × top shade × AO`. Assert directly; this is not a golden.
- **LOD0 fade:** a long flat LOD0 grass strip running away from the camera. Near pixels match the textured golden-style look, far pixels match the flat average within ±2 LSB, and the transition is monotonic along the strip.
- **Fog:** with fog on, a terrain pixel at distance ≥ the fog end equals the sky color behind it within ±1 LSB, and a near pixel is unchanged.
- **No visible border:** in the rings scene, with no fog, the mean color in a 16×16 window just inside and just outside the LOD0/LOD1 border differs by less than 3% in luminance on flat ground.
  - Assert that directly for the `zoom_border01` and `down_border01` cameras.
  - Hole tests (the pinhole rules) still pass, with fog on.
- **Goldens.** This task changes how terrain looks, so regenerate **every** golden that shows terrain:
  - `terrain_spawn`, `edit_spawn`, `mesh_*` and the three `rings_*`;
  - `sky_*` and `debug_overlay` only if they change.
  - List each regenerated golden in the report, with a sentence on what changed.
  - Keep the old images for the orchestrator to compare side by side: copy them to `target/golden_before/`, which is not committed.
  - Do not change the golden tolerance.
