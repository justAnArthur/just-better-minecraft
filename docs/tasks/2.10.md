# 2.10 LOD on tall terrain: surface-following windows, level tint, farther rings

**Goal:** on the 2.8 mountains, full-detail terrain is drawn everywhere near the player, coarse levels only far away, and the far view reaches further. The user play-tested wave D (2.8 + 2.9) and found:

1. **Coarse LOD drawn right next to the player.** A flat-colored 16-block wall in front of the player standing in a valley, and coarse blocks in the valley under a flying player. Cause: LOD0 streams a fixed vertical window (`vertical_radius` 4 cubes = ±128 blocks around the camera). Terrain now spans y 23..409, so near terrain above or below the window is covered by LOD1+ (the coverage rule draws the finest ready level). The LOD rings have the same fixed window (`lod_vertical_radius` 3).
2. **Blue triangles at the coast** seen from altitude (water triangles cutting into the sand beach). Not yet diagnosed.
3. **"LOD is not very accurate":** coarse terrain sits visibly off the real surface.
4. Wants the **LOD to reach further**, and wants to **see the levels** ("show me the stages, 2x2 etc").

(A fifth report, dark-green lines on far sand, was the skirt layer bit leaking into the face layer; fixed on the wave branch in 4200594.)

## Scope

### A. Level tint debug view (client) — do this first

- A key (**F4**) and `JBM_LOD_TINT=1` toggle a tint that multiplies each fragment by a fixed color per LOD level: LOD0 untinted, L1 (2×2×2 cells) red, L2 (4×4) yellow, L3 (8×8) green, L4 (16×16) cyan, L5 blue, L6 magenta-free purple (never pure magenta: the hole sentinel). One uniform flag; the same on all three draw paths.
- The debug overlay (F3) shows the legend: `L1 2x2 red, L2 4x4 yellow, …`.
- Use it to diagnose B and C, and include tinted screenshots in the report.

### B. Surface-following vertical windows (server)

Replace the fixed vertical window, for LOD0 and for every ring level, with: **the camera's vertical window ∪ the column's surface span**.
- For each cube column `(x, z)` in a level's horizontal disk, the surface span is the range of cubes that contain the terrain surface over that column's footprint: `[floor(min_surface / side), floor(max_surface / side)]`, widened by one cube below (so the surface cube's -Y neighbor is there for meshing and skirts) — min/max over the footprint's columns from the generator's heightmap (the band fast path; cache per column, it is deterministic per seed and preset).
- The camera window stays (`vertical_radius`, `lod_vertical_radius`) so a flying or digging player still has what is around them, and edits keep working.
- Ordering: keep nearest-first by distance, the per-tick budgets, the keep-set hysteresis (+1 cube) and the unload rules. Precompute what can be precomputed; the per-column span lookup must stay cheap at game scale.
- Classic and tests: with `Classic` terrain (36..92) and the existing configs, the desired sets of existing tests must not change in meaning; if they change, explain each.
- **Cap:** at most `max_column_cubes` (config, default 16) per column per level beyond the camera window, nearest to the camera first.
- Measure at game scale (`JBM_REGION_SIDE=544`): cubes desired per level before/after at spawn and on a mountain, generation time to settle, and memory.

### C. Coast triangles

Reproduce the blue triangles with a test camera looking at a coast from altitude on the overworld (seed `DEFAULT_SEED`), using the tint view to see which level draws them. Find the cause and fix it. Add a test that asserts the defect is gone (pixels or geometry, whichever is direct). If B makes them disappear, show why (tinted before/after) and still add the test.

### D. Farther rings

- Raise the default `lod_levels` from 4 to 6 (L5 cells 32 blocks, L6 cells 64 blocks; the outer ring reaches about 16 km). Fog and fade read the outermost ring radius from the uniform, so they follow; check.
- If 6 is too expensive (frame time, ring fill time, memory, server tick), pick 5 and say why with numbers.
- Report: cubes per level, ring fill time at spawn (threaded), frame time (`terrain_draw` rings bench if it covers it, and the autopilot summary from the orchestrator).

### E. Accuracy

- Measure, per level 1..=6, the height error of the coarse surface vs the true surface on the overworld (mean and p99 of `coarse_top − true_top` over a seeded sample), and include it in the report.
- The downsampling rule leans solid (a cell is filled when 2 of its 8 children are), so coarse terrain sits up to 2^n − 1 blocks high; the skirts rely on that bias. **Do not change the rule in this task** unless you prove (with the hole tests and lod_fly) that a less biased rule leaves no cracks. If you have a safe improvement, implement it; otherwise propose it in the report.
- After B, coarse levels only draw far away, which is most of the visible inaccuracy. Confirm with tinted screenshots.

### F. Beaches

The 2.8 wave fix made sand of every column with `h ≤ SEA_LEVEL + 1`; flat lowland at sea level became huge sand plains. Keep beaches narrow: tune the overworld so lowland rises off the sea level near coasts (spline or rule), and assert in a test that **sand columns are under 5%** of land columns in the 8 km shape sample, while coasts still show sand.
