# 2.8 Dramatic worldgen: continents, mountain ranges, valleys

**Goal:** the default world is worth looking at from far away.
- Continents and oceans at the kilometre scale.
- Ridged mountain ranges a few hundred blocks tall, with valleys between them, plateaus and rolling lowlands.
- Coordinates are domain-warped, so ridges bend and coasts wander instead of looking like plain noise.

Today's terrain spans only 36–92 blocks, which leaves the LOD rings from 2.6 and 2.7 nothing to show. Reference look: the Terrain Diffusion mod for Minecraft (big continental shapes, sharp ridgelines, wide valleys). We do not use a learned model. We author a richer density graph in the 2.1 format and add the few nodes it needs.

## Scope

### New graph nodes (`graph.rs`; extend compile, eval, bounds and tests for each)

- **Domain warp.** `noise2` and `fbm2` gain optional `x_offset` and `z_offset` operands (`NodeValue`, default 0). They sample at `((x + x_offset)·x_scale, (z + z_offset)·z_scale)`. Offsets may reference other nodes, typically noise × a few hundred blocks.
  - Bounds: noise bounds stay valid whatever the offset, so an offset never widens them.
  - Graphs without offsets must compile to the same instructions as before, with the same fingerprint as now. Assert both.
- **`ridged2`:** ridged multifractal over the reference noise, with `salt`, `scale`, `octaves`, `gain` and optional offsets. Per octave `1 − |n|`, squared, weighted by the previous octave (the classic Musgrave form), normalized to `[0, 1]`.
  - Document the formula.
  - Exact interval bounds `[0, 1]`.
  - A reference implementation in `worldgen::reference` for the equivalence tests.
- Anything else you need: the smallest addition that does the job, with bounds. Splines, clamp, min/max and abs already exist.
- Every node stays a 2D (column) node. The density remains `surface(x, z) − y`. So the band/heightmap fast path, the `bounds` fast paths and `sample_lod` keep working. **No 3D noise, overhangs or caves in this task.**

### The new overworld (`assets/worldgen/overworld.json`)

Build these layers, each with a role you document in a comment block at the top of the README section:

1. **Continentalness:** a very low-frequency fbm over a few kilometres, warped. Through a spline it maps to the base height: deep ocean floor well below sea level, coasts, lowlands, highlands.
2. **Erosion / ruggedness:** a second low-frequency field. It decides how much of the mountain field applies, so ranges sit in bands and plains stay plain.
3. **Mountains:** `ridged2`, warped, scaled by ruggedness and by continentalness (no mountains in oceans). Peaks reach about **+250 to +350** blocks above sea level in the roughest areas.
4. **Valleys / rivers-lite:** `1 − ridged`-style or abs-noise channels that carve wide valleys through the highlands. No water simulation; valleys below sea level fill with water (below).
5. **Detail:** a small-amplitude fbm for local roughness, stronger on mountains than on plains.
6. **Plateaus (optional):** a spline step on mid-height ranges.

Rules:
- Heights are integers (`floor`), as now.
- The graph's own interval bounds must prove the full height range, and that range must fit the band (`MAX_BAND_ROWS` = 512). Choose a sea level (about 64) and keep min/max within 512 rows.
- Derive `MIN_HEIGHT` and `MAX_HEIGHT` from the graph's bounds instead of hard-coding them, or update them and assert they match the bounds.

### Water and surface (`terrain.rs`)

- **Sea level.** Add a `SEA_LEVEL` constant. Air cells at or below it become `base:water` in the surface stage, in both `generate` and `sample_lod`. Water is non-solid, so physics already passes through it.
- **Beaches:** sand at the coasts near sea level, as `SAND_LEVEL` does now, tied to `SEA_LEVEL`.
- **Altitude materials:** stone on steep slopes and above about sea level + 180, so peaks are bare rock. There is no snow block yet; snow needs a block and a texture and is a follow-up.
  - The material must stay local: decided per column from that column's own surface, so it is cheap and LOD-consistent.
  - Document the rules.
- **Spawn:** search outward from (0, 0) in a fixed spiral for the first land column above sea level with a gentle slope, and spawn there. Deterministic per seed. Assert spawn is on land in a test for several seeds.

- **Every test, bench and example that depends on today's terrain** pins `Classic`: the terrain and edit screenshot goldens, `edit_e2e`, `prediction`, the server LOD and streaming tests that assume specific heights, testkit, and the worldgen equivalence tests. Then **no golden changes** and those tests keep their meaning.

  - `lod_fly` uses the new `Overworld`: it is the real-flight check.
- The game binary uses `Overworld`.
- Stored worlds: the 2.7 worldgen fingerprint already invalidates stored LOD. LOD0 records of an existing world made with the classic graph stay as they are. Document that old worlds keep their edited cubes but generate new terrain around them.

## Tests

- **Node equivalence:** domain-warped `noise2`/`fbm2` and `ridged2` match their reference implementations at 10,000 seeded points, including offsets that reference other nodes.
- **Bounds are sound:** for every new node and for the full overworld graph, sampled values over 100,000 seeded columns, including `|x|, |z|` up to 2^30, stay inside the interval bounds. The overworld's proven height range is printed and fits the band.
- **Shape statistics** over a seeded 8 km × 8 km sample at 16-block spacing. Assert with sensible bounds, and print the actual numbers:
  - ocean fraction between 20% and 50%;
  - the 99th-percentile height at least sea level + 200;
  - the median land height within sea level + 5 … + 60, so most land is walkable lowland or hills;
  - at least one connected mountain region spanning ≥ 2 km.
- **Water:** a column whose surface is below sea level has water from surface+1 up to `SEA_LEVEL`, and air above. Same at LOD1 from `sample_lod`.
- **LOD consistency:** 2.7's level-1 exactness test and the levels 2–4 agreement test pass on the new graph. Report the numbers; mountains make the ±1-cell agreement harder.
- **Spawn on land** for 10 seeds.
- **Preview:** a new example, `crates/server/examples/worldgen_preview.rs`. It writes a top-down PNG of an N km square, coloured by height and water with hillshading, using the image crate the workspace already depends on (check; add nothing new).
  - Produce previews for 3 seeds at 8 km. Put them under `target/worldgen_preview/` (not committed) and list them in the report. The orchestrator inspects them.
- All existing tests pass unchanged in meaning (pinned to `Classic`), and goldens are unchanged.

## Out of scope

- 3D density, overhangs, caves, arches.
- Biomes beyond height and slope materials. Trees, ores and structures.
- Rivers as flowing water or water simulation.
- Rendering water as translucent; whatever the renderer does today is fine, and 2.9 handles far colours.
- Learned or diffusion terrain models.
